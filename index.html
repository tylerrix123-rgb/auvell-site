A short guide

What you are looking at

What a peptide is

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. Proteins are the same idea, only much longer. Your body already makes many peptides. The ones on this list are laboratory copies, made so a researcher can study one sequence at a time.

What they are studied for

In a lab they are used to look at how cells pass signals. Different sequences are interesting for different questions. This site does not say what a person should take, how much, or how. It is not medical advice, and nothing here is for human or veterinary use.

Where the material comes from

It is made by chemical synthesis, then dried. It is not taken from a person or an animal. What you see in the vial is a small amount of dry powder, often called a lyophilised cake, sealed under a cap.

How the powder is kept

Leave the cap on until the material is needed. Keep the vial upright, dry, and out of bright light and damp air. A warm shelf, a sunny window, or a bathroom is a poor place for it. Do not open a vial just to look inside.

Temperature

These are the temperatures research labs usually use for a sealed, dry vial. They are not a guarantee for every compound. Ask us about the one on your note.

  • −20°C freezer — the normal place for an unopened dry vial you are keeping for weeks or months.
  • −80°C freezer — used when a dry vial needs to be held for a long time, often many months to a few years.
  • 2–8°C fridge — fine for a short spell, roughly days up to a few weeks, if the cap stays on and the vial stays dry.
  • About 15–25°C, room temperature — only while a vial is in transit or sitting out briefly. Not a storage place. Do not leave it overnight on a bench.
  • Above about 30°C, or in sun — avoid. Heat and bright light are what spoil the powder fastest.

Put the vial in the body of the freezer, not the door, so the temperature does not swing each time the door opens. Let a cold vial sit sealed until it is no longer fogged with condensation before you open it, so moisture from the air does not land in the powder.

After it is opened

A sealed dry vial is the stable form. Once the seal is broken, moisture gets in and the powder does not keep as well, even in the freezer.

If liquid has already been added, labs usually keep that vial at 2–8°C and use it within a few days, or freeze portions at −20°C. Thaw a portion once. Freezing and thawing the same liquid again and again is what to avoid. This page does not describe how to mix a vial. Ask that on WhatsApp for the compound you mean.
